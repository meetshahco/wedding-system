# Master Creative & Animation Brief
## Pujan weds Ria — Interactive Wedding Invitation Opening Experience

### 1. Overall vision

Create a premium, cinematic, photorealistic opening experience for a wedding invitation website.

The experience should feel like receiving a beautifully handcrafted physical wedding invitation, made with luxurious ivory paper, intricate embossed floral patterns, delicate gold detailing and a personalised wax/metallic seal.

The visitor should feel invited to physically interact with the envelope before entering the wedding website.

This is not a conventional website intro, splash screen or generic animation. It should feel like a carefully art-directed luxury stationery film translated into an interactive web experience.

The visual language should be:
- Elegant, warm, understated and sophisticated.
- Inspired by premium Indian wedding stationery.
- Predominantly ivory, champagne gold and warm cream.
- Accented with muted rose pink and sage green, derived from the original PR monogram.
- Photorealistic in material, lighting, shadows and physical movement.
- Cinematic, but not overly dramatic or theatrical.

The animation must feel tactile and believable, as though the visitor is interacting with a real physical invitation.

Use the supplied reference images as the primary visual direction.

**Reference A — Envelope design**

The logo should look physically integrated into the seal, with appropriate surface depth, highlights and shadows, without altering the original artwork.

# 4. Detailed animation sequence

Target duration: approximately 10–13 seconds, excluding the time the visitor spends deciding to open the invitation.

The animation should be divided into clearly distinguishable stages.

## SCENE 01 — The invitation appears

Duration: 0–1.5 seconds.

The screen opens to a beautifully framed ivory wedding envelope.

Composition:
- The envelope occupies most of the viewport.
- It is positioned centrally.
- The camera looks at it almost directly, with a very subtle perspective that reveals its physical depth.
- The envelope is surrounded by a soft warm ivory environment.
- The lighting is diffused, like natural daylight entering a premium stationery studio.

The envelope should feel like a real object placed on a beautiful surface.

Introduce a very subtle camera movement or depth parallax, almost imperceptible.

The embossed floral and lotus patterns should initially be visible but understated.

Do not introduce unnecessary particles, floating objects or decorative elements that are not part of the physical envelope.

## SCENE 02 — Light awakens the embossing

Duration: 1.5–3.5 seconds.

This is an important visual moment.

A soft, warm champagne-gold light begins travelling across the envelope.

The lighting should reveal the embossed patterns progressively.

As the light moves:
- Raised floral details catch subtle highlights.
- Lotus patterns become more visible.
- Fine embossed borders develop gentle highlights.
- The braided gold border catches realistic metallic reflections.
- The paper texture becomes more apparent through grazing light.

The light should feel like illumination travelling over a physical surface, not like a digital glowing outline.

Introduce extremely subtle rose-pink and sage-green reflections in selected floral details, inspired by the PR monogram.

These colours must remain secondary to ivory and gold.

The effect should be restrained and luxurious.

Do not make the entire envelope glow simultaneously.

Do not use neon effects, strong bloom filters, sparkling particles or exaggerated magical effects.

## SCENE 03 — The seal becomes the focal point

Duration: 3.5–4.5 seconds.

The lighting gradually draws attention towards the central seal.

The seal should become the visual focal point.

It must look like a real handcrafted medallion:
- Circular and dimensional.
- Fine engraved or embossed detailing around its perimeter.
- Realistic metallic and ivory material interaction.
- Soft natural contact shadows.
- Subtle reflections.
- Original PR monogram faithfully integrated in the centre.

The monogram must remain unchanged.

The seal should feel like a substantial physical object attached to the envelope.

Introduce a very subtle highlight across the logo and outer rim.

The CTA should feel naturally integrated into the composition.

Possible microcopy:

"Tap the seal to open"

Keep it understated, elegant and unobtrusive.

The seal itself is the primary interactive element.

## SCENE 04 — The visitor lifts the seal

Duration: 4.5–5.5 seconds after interaction.

The visitor taps the seal.

This triggers the opening sequence.

CRITICAL PHYSICAL BEHAVIOUR:

The seal must NOT break, crack, split, tear or separate into two pieces.

It must move upwards as one complete, intact object.

The movement should resemble a person gently lifting a physical medallion from an envelope.

Animation:
1. The seal responds to the tap with a subtle physical press or tactile feedback.
2. A small shadow shift indicates that it is detaching from the paper.
3. The seal lifts vertically upwards.
4. It maintains its complete circular shape and original design.
5. It moves slightly away from the envelope surface.
6. Its shadow changes naturally as it gains distance from the paper.
7. It may rotate by only a few degrees if necessary to create realistic depth, but must remain recognisable and visually stable.

The movement should feel gentle and deliberate.

Do not make it fly away, spin dramatically, explode or dissolve.

The seal must remain intact throughout the sequence.

The original PR monogram should remain visible during this movement.

After lifting, the seal can move slightly towards the upper portion of the screen and out of the primary composition.

Do not make the logo disappear through a destructive effect.

## SCENE 05 — The envelope opens

Duration: 5.5–7 seconds.

Once the seal has lifted, the envelope flap begins opening.

The opening must follow believable physical mechanics.

The upper flap should lift and rotate around its natural fold.

Important physical details:
- The paper has thickness.
- The flap casts a soft shadow on the inner envelope.
- The inner surface is slightly darker before light enters.
- The fold moves smoothly without distortion.
- The embossed patterns remain attached to the paper surface.
- The gold border follows the movement naturally.

The flap should not disappear or dissolve.

As it opens, a warm champagne-gold light emerges from within.

This light should be soft and inviting, as though the inside of the invitation is illuminated.

The illumination should gradually become stronger towards the centre of the envelope.

Use subtle rose-pink and sage-green colour hints in the reflected light.

Avoid an intense white flash.

The visitor should feel that something beautiful is waiting inside.

## SCENE 06 — The lotus bud is revealed

Duration: 7–8.5 seconds.

This is the transition's most important visual bridge.

A closed pink lotus bud emerges from the warm illuminated interior of the envelope.

The lotus should be recognisably connected to the original landing page's lotus design.

Visual characteristics:
- Soft blush-pink petals.
- Fine natural petal veins.
- Slightly translucent petal edges.
- Delicate natural shading.
- A subtle warm highlight along the petal surfaces.
- Realistic organic form.

The bud should initially remain closed.

Do not reveal the fully bloomed flower yet.

The camera begins a gentle forward movement towards the lotus.

The envelope's embossed patterns and gold detailing gradually become softer and less prominent.

The warm internal light transitions into the pale ivory-yellow atmosphere of the website.

This should feel like moving from the physical invitation into the world represented by the invitation.

## SCENE 07 — Seamless transition into the original website

Duration: 8.5–10 seconds.

Transition from the close-up lotus bud into the original landing page.

The transition should be seamless.

The camera movement should create the illusion that the visitor is entering the lotus world.

As the camera pulls into the next scene:
- The closed lotus bud becomes the central subject.
- The original website background becomes visible.
- The surrounding environment gradually reveals itself.
- The green lily pads appear.
- Small decorative flowers and lotus elements become visible.
- Hanging bells appear at the top.
- The original website's composition is restored.

IMPORTANT:

The original landing page must begin with the closed lotus bud.

The lotus should not suddenly jump to an open state during the transition.

Match the position, scale, perspective, colour and lighting of the lotus between the transition scene and the original website scene.

Ideally, use the same lotus model or animation asset in both scenes so that there is no visible mismatch.

The camera movement should be continuous enough to make the transition feel like one uninterrupted experience.

Avoid a hard cut, abrupt zoom or generic fade-to-white.

# 5. Original landing page — Lotus blooming

Duration: approximately 10–13 seconds onward.

Once the original landing page is established, the lotus blooming animation begins.

This section should retain the original website's existing visual identity.

Do not redesign the page.

The sequence:

**Stage A — Closed bud**

The lotus is centred in the composition.

It is initially closed, with the same appearance as the original reference.

The environment is warm, soft and calm.

**Stage B — Initial opening**

The outer petals begin separating gently.

The movement should be organic and slightly asynchronous.

Not every petal should move at exactly the same time.

The petals should have subtle individual movement and natural curvature.

**Stage C — Full bloom**

The inner petals gradually open.

The lotus expands into its original full-bloom appearance.

Use realistic overlapping petals, subtle translucency and delicate shading.

The opening should feel slow, graceful and peaceful.

Do not make the petals snap open or rotate mechanically.

**Stage D — Landing page settles**

As the flower reaches its final bloom:
- Lily pads settle into their original positions.
- Smaller lotus elements become visible.
- A few delicate petals drift gently.
- The hanging bells remain suspended with only subtle natural movement.
- The lighting stabilises.
- The original heading and supporting text become visible in their original layout.

Retain the original text:

"Marriages are settled in heaven but celebrated on earth"

"Pujan weds Ria"

"❤️ हमारी PURI कहानी ❤️"

And the existing instruction:

"Scroll to reveal the date"

Do not invent new copy or alter the original content.

The final state should match the supplied website reference.

# 6. Lighting and atmosphere

Lighting is one of the most important aspects of this experience.

The lighting should create a continuous visual connection between the envelope and the lotus world.

Use three lighting characteristics:

**A. Grazing light**

Used on the envelope to reveal embossed patterns and paper texture.

**B. Metallic reflections**

Used on the gold border, seal and other metallic details.

**C. Soft ambient glow**

Used during the opening to create a warm transition into the lotus scene.

The light should travel with a clear direction and purpose.

It should not flicker randomly.

The pink and sage accents should be subtle reflections, not coloured spotlights.

Use soft shadows, realistic ambient occlusion and restrained highlights.

Avoid:
- Excessive lens flares.
- Large glowing halos.
- Strong volumetric light beams.
- Excessive glitter.
- Random sparkles.
- Overexposed surfaces.
- Artificial neon-like outlines.

The objective is understated luxury.

# 7. Sound design

The experience should support a gentle Indian instrumental soundtrack.

Primary instrument: bamboo flute.

Suggested supporting instruments:
- Very soft tanpura ambience.
- Delicate Indian bells.
- Subtle atmospheric textures.

Sound progression:

1. Initial envelope appearance: quiet ambient atmosphere.
2. Light travelling across embossing: barely audible delicate shimmer.
3. Seal interaction: subtle tactile metallic/paper sound.
4. Seal lifting: a soft physical movement sound.
5. Envelope opening: gentle paper movement.
6. Lotus reveal: a delicate flute phrase begins to emerge.
7. Lotus blooming: flute becomes slightly more prominent.
8. Landing page: soundtrack settles into a calm instrumental loop.

Do not use dramatic cinematic orchestral music.

Do not use loud temple bells or overly devotional sound effects.

The audio must feel calm, romantic and distinctly Indian.

Sound should begin only after the visitor interacts, respecting browser autoplay restrictions.

Provide an accessible mute/unmute control consistent with the original website.

# 8. Interaction and CTA behaviour

The opening sequence should be user-initiated.

Initial state:
- Envelope is visible.
- The seal is prominent.
- The CTA is subtly displayed.
- The rest of the composition remains calm.

On tapping the seal:
- Trigger the complete opening sequence.
- Prevent accidental repeated interactions.
- Do not require multiple clicks to complete the opening.
- Do not allow the seal to be broken or moved independently by the user.
- Complete the transition automatically.

Once the internal page is revealed, normal website interactions should become available.

The experience should also support:
- Desktop mouse interaction.
- Mobile touch interaction.
- Responsive positioning.
- Keyboard-accessible CTA.
- Appropriate reduced-motion behaviour.

# 9. Technical implementation requirements

Before implementation, inspect the existing project and understand its architecture.

Do not replace the existing landing page or rebuild the website from scratch.

The opening experience must be added as a separate introductory layer that transitions into the existing website.

Suggested architecture:

**Layer 1 — Envelope intro**
- Full viewport.
- Independent animation state.
- Envelope and seal assets.
- Interactive seal CTA.

**Layer 2 — Transition**
- Controlled flap opening.
- Lighting progression.
- Lotus bud reveal.
- Camera transition.

**Layer 3 — Existing landing page**
- Preserve the current layout.
- Preserve the existing lotus bloom animation.
- Preserve all existing text and elements.
- Preserve existing scroll behaviour.

Use reusable animation states rather than independent animations that can become unsynchronised.

Suggested states:

1. `envelope_idle`
2. `embossed_light_reveal`
3. `seal_interaction`
4. `seal_lift`
5. `envelope_open`
6. `lotus_bud_reveal`
7. `page_transition`
8. `lotus_bloom`
9. `landing_page_ready`

The transitions should be controlled and deterministic.

Do not use arbitrary timeouts that can cause elements to animate out of sync.

The animation should be responsive and maintain visual quality on mobile devices.

Prioritise:
- Smooth frame rates.
- Optimised textures.
- Efficient rendering.
- Controlled GPU usage.
- Minimal layout shifts.
- No unnecessary DOM re-renders.
- No excessive video or texture downloads.

If using 3D, use it only where it materially improves the physical realism of the envelope, seal or lotus.

Do not introduce a heavy 3D engine unnecessarily.

If the existing lotus is already implemented in Three.js, WebGL, CSS or another animation system, assess whether its existing implementation can be reused.

Keep the experience compatible with the existing deployment setup.

Provide graceful fallbacks for devices that cannot handle advanced effects.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by offering a simplified transition without forced camera movement or lengthy animation.

The final result should feel like opening a beautifully handcrafted wedding invitation and discovering a delicate, living lotus garden inside.

### The emotional objective

The visitor should experience three distinct feelings:

1. **Anticipation** — Something beautiful is waiting inside the envelope.
2. **Discovery** — The seal lifts, the invitation opens and a warm light reveals the lotus.
3. **Delight** — The closed lotus gradually blooms, welcoming the visitor into Pujan and Ria's wedding world.

Make every movement intentional. Let the materials, light and physical details do the storytelling.

The experience should be elegant enough that it feels special even when viewed without sound.
